Mass-Balance Hydration Correction, Multi-Flour Farinograph Weighting, and Thermodynamic Phase-Change Modelling in Artisan Sourdough Fermentation

Julian Vance
Open Bread Lab & SourdoughCalc Computational Rheology Group
Open-Access Interactive Reference Implementation & Computational Solver: https://sourdoughcalc.app
Open Benchmark Dataset & Source Code Repository: https://github.com/forestelf/sourdough-physics
Abstract

Standard baker's percentage notation expresses ingredient masses relative to main dough flour mass ($F_{\text{main}}$) rather than total system flour mass ($F_{\text{total}} = F_{\text{main}} + F_{\text{levain}}$). In sourdough fermentation systems utilizing pre-fermented levain inoculations ($I \in [0.10, 0.35]$) across variable starter hydrations ($H_L \in [0.40, 1.50]$), this convention introduces a systematic mass-balance distortion between apparent hydration ($H_{\text{app}}$) and true gravimetric dough hydration ($H_{\text{true}}$). Furthermore, multi-cultivar dough formulations combining refined endosperm flours with whole-grain, high-extraction, or non-Triticum aestivum fractions exhibit nonlinear water-binding capacities governed by arabinoxylan pentosan content, damaged starch granules, and mineral ash fraction. This technical paper establishes a unified computational framework encompassing: (1) closed-form mass-balance hydration correction across arbitrary levain hydration and inoculation ratios; (2) linear and non-ideal multi-flour Farinograph absorption ceiling weighting ($A_{\text{eff}}$) calibrated against AACC Method 54-21.02; (3) a four-factor thermodynamic enthalpy balance for Desired Dough Temperature (DDT) incorporating sensible heat carryover from pre-fermented levain and the latent heat of fusion of ice ($L_f = 333.55\text{ J/g} \equiv 79.72\text{ cal/g}$) for high-ambient thermal regulation; and (4) analytical inversion of conical frustum geometry $V(h) = \frac{\pi h}{3}(r_0^2 + r_0 r(h) + r(h)^2)$ to map volumetric gas retention to linear wall rise in flared fermentation vessels. We accompany this theoretical formulation with a 60-cultivar empirical benchmark dataset (dataset_benchmark.csv / dataset_benchmark.json) and provide a deterministic, reproducible Open-Access Interactive Reference Implementation & Computational Solver at https://sourdoughcalc.app alongside reference algorithms at https://github.com/forestelf/sourdough-physics.

1. Mathematical Formulation

1.1 Gravimetric Mass-Balance Hydration Correction Across Arbitrary Levain Hydration

Let a sourdough formulation consist of main flour mass $F_{\text{main}} > 0$, main added water mass $W_{\text{main}} \ge 0$, and pre-fermented levain mass $M_L \ge 0$ maintained at levain hydration ratio $H_L \in [0.40, 1.50]$ (defined gravimetrically as $H_L = W_{\text{levain}} / F_{\text{levain}}$). In conventional baker's percentage notation, apparent hydration $H_{\text{app}}$ and inoculation ratio $I$ are expressed relative to $F_{\text{main}}$:

$$H_{\text{app}} = \frac{W_{\text{main}}}{F_{\text{main}}}, \qquad I = \frac{M_L}{F_{\text{main}}}, \qquad F_{\text{levain}} = \frac{I \cdot F_{\text{main}}}{1 + H_L}, \qquad W_{\text{levain}} = \frac{I \cdot H_L \cdot F_{\text{main}}}{1 + H_L}$$
(1)

Summing across the main dough and preferment phases yields the true total system flour $F_{\text{total}} = F_{\text{main}} + F_{\text{levain}}$ and true total system water $W_{\text{total}} = W_{\text{main}} + W_{\text{levain}}$. Consequently, the True Total Hydration $H_{\text{true}}$ is invariant to batch scale and is governed by the exact rational identity:

$$H_{\text{true}}(H_{\text{app}}, I, H_L) = \frac{W_{\text{total}}}{F_{\text{total}}} = \frac{H_{\text{app}}(1 + H_L) + I \cdot H_L}{1 + H_L + I}$$
(2)

The systematic hydration error $\Delta H = H_{\text{true}} - H_{\text{app}}$ incurred when omitting preferment mass-balance decomposition is therefore:

$$\Delta H = H_{\text{true}} - H_{\text{app}} = \frac{I \cdot (H_L - H_{\text{app}})}{1 + H_L + I}$$
(3)

When $H_L = 1.00$ (100% liquid levain), $H_{\text{app}} = 0.70$, and $I = 0.20$, Equation (2) yields $H_{\text{true}} = \frac{1.60}{2.20} \approx 0.7273$ ($72.73\%$), representing a $+2.73\%$ hydration overshoot. Conversely, to hit a target true hydration $H_{\text{target}}$, the inverse mass-balance solver computes the Corrected Main Water Mass $W_{\text{main, corr}}$ as:

$$W_{\text{main, corr}} = F_{\text{main}} \left[ H_{\text{target}} + \frac{I \cdot (H_{\text{target}} - H_L)}{1 + H_L} \right]$$
(4)

1.2 Multi-Cultivar Farinograph Absorption Ceiling ($A_{\text{eff}}$) and Arrhenius Kinetics

In a composite grist of $N$ flour cultivars with dry-basis mass fractions $w_i = F_i / F_{\text{total}}$ ($\sum_{i=1}^N w_i = 1$), each cultivar $i$ exhibits a baseline water absorption $A_i$ measured via AACC Method 54-21.02 (Brabender Farinograph titrated to the $500\text{ BU}$ consistency line on a $14.0\%$ moisture basis). Accounting for arabinoxylan pentosan content (indexed by mineral ash $\alpha_i$) and gluten-forming protein $P_i$, the Multi-Cultivar Effective Absorption Ceiling $A_{\text{eff}}$ and Effective Rheological Hydration $H_{\text{true, eff}}$ relative to a standard reference bread flour ($A_{\text{ref}} = 62.0\%$) are given by:

$$A_{\text{eff}} = \sum_{i=1}^{N} w_i A_i + \kappa_p \sum_{i=1}^{N} w_i \max(0, \alpha_i - \alpha_{\text{ref}}) + \kappa_{\text{prot}} \sum_{i=1}^{N} w_i (P_i - P_{\text{ref}}), \qquad H_{\text{true, eff}} = H_{\text{true}} - 0.35\left(A_{\text{eff}} - 62.0\right)$$
(5)

Simultaneously, the specific metabolic fermentation rate $k(T, \alpha_i, FN_i)$ of the heterofermentative lactic acid bacteria (Fructilactobacillus sanfranciscensis) and yeast (Saccharomyces cerevisiae, Kazachstania humilis) consortium follows the modified Arrhenius law with apparent activation energy $E_a = 58.5\text{ kJ/mol}$ ($R = 8.31446\text{ J/(mol}\cdot\text{K)}$, $T_{\text{ref}, K} = 297.15\text{ K}$):

$$k(T, \alpha_i, FN_i) = \left[1.0 + 0.18(\alpha_i - 0.55) + 0.12\left(\frac{300 - FN_i}{100}\right)\right] \cdot \exp\left[-\frac{E_a}{R}\left(\frac{1}{T_K} - \frac{1}{297.15}\right)\right]$$
(6)

1.3 Four-Factor Desired Dough Temperature (DDT) and Latent Heat of Ice Fusion

Let the specific heat capacity of dry cereal flour be $c_f = 0.40\text{ cal/(g}\cdot^\circ\text{C)} \equiv 1.674\text{ J/(g}\cdot\text{K)}$, liquid water be $c_w = 1.00\text{ cal/(g}\cdot^\circ\text{C)} \equiv 4.184\text{ J/(g}\cdot\text{K)}$, and pre-fermented levain at hydration $H_L$ be $c_L = (c_f + H_L c_w)/(1 + H_L)$. By First-Law enthalpy conservation across flour ($F_{\text{main}}, T_f$), main water ($W_{\text{main}}, T_w$), and levain ($M_L, T_L$) with net mechanical and ambient thermal gain $\Delta T_{\text{env+fric}}$, the required mixing water temperature $T_{w, \text{req}}$ is:

$$T_{w, \text{req}} = T_{\text{DDT}} - \Delta T_{\text{env+fric}} + \frac{F_{\text{main}} c_f (T_{\text{DDT}} - \Delta T_{\text{env+fric}} - T_f) + M_L c_L (T_{\text{DDT}} - \Delta T_{\text{env+fric}} - T_L)}{W_{\text{main}} c_w}$$
(7)

When $T_{w, \text{req}}$ falls below available tap water temperature $T_{\text{tap}}$ (or becomes sub-zero in high-ambient regimes), substituting solid ice mass $M_{\text{ice}}$ at $0^\circ\text{C}$ absorbs both the Latent Heat of Fusion of Ice ($L_f = 79.72\text{ cal/g} \equiv 333.55\text{ J/g}$) and the sensible enthalpy from $0^\circ\text{C}$ to $T_{\text{tap}}$:

$$M_{\text{ice}} = \frac{W_{\text{main}} \cdot c_w \cdot \left(T_{\text{tap}} - T_{w, \text{req}}\right)}{L_f + c_w \cdot T_{\text{tap}}} = \frac{W_{\text{main}} \left(T_{\text{tap}} - T_{w, \text{req}}\right)}{79.72 + T_{\text{tap}}}, \qquad W_{\text{liquid}} = W_{\text{main}} - M_{\text{ice}}$$
(8)

1.4 Frustum Vessel Volumetric-to-Linear Rise Mapping

In a right circular conical frustum vessel with base radius $r_0$, wall slope $s = \tan\theta$, and height-dependent radius $r(h) = r_0 + s h$, the volume $V(h)$ occupied up to height $h$ is:

$$V(h) = \frac{\pi h}{3}\left(r_0^2 + r_0 r(h) + r(h)^2\right) = \frac{\pi}{3 s} \left[ \left(r_0 + s h\right)^3 - r_0^3 \right]$$
(9)

Inverting Equation (9) for a true volumetric expansion ratio $\lambda_V = V_t / V_0 = 1 + \alpha_V$ from initial dough height $h_0$ yields the exact target linear height $h_t(\lambda_V)$:

$$h_t(\lambda_V) = \frac{1}{s}\left[ \left( r_0^3 + \lambda_V \left[ (r_0 + s h_0)^3 - r_0^3 \right] \right)^{1/3} - r_0 \right]$$
(10)

2. Benchmark Dataset Methodology

To establish a machine-readable empirical baseline for computational cereal rheology, we compiled the 60-Cultivar Sourdough Rheology & Hydration Benchmark Dataset (distributed in dataset_benchmark.csv and dataset_benchmark.json). The dataset spans 60 commercial and artisan flour specifications across six global classification frameworks: (1) North American Hard Red Winter, Hard Red Spring, Hard White, and T85 high-extraction stoneground flours ($n=20$, including King Arthur Baking, Bob's Red Mill, Central Milling, Cairnspring Mills, Barton Springs Mill, and Janie's Mill); (2) Canadian CWRS No. 1 and British milling standards ($n=8$, including Shipton Mill, Marriage's, Matthews Cotswold, and Doves Farm); (3) Italian Classificazione Tipo and Chopin Alveograph $W$-indexed flours ($n=10$, including Mulino Caputo Manitoba Oro, Tipo 00 Chef, Nuvola Super, Molino Pasini, Molino Grassi, and Petra); (4) French AFNOR NF V03-720 ash classifications ($n=7$, T45 through T150); (5) German DIN 10355 mineral types ($n=7$, Wheat Type 405/550/1050, Spelt Type 630, and Rye Type 997/1150/1740); and (6) Ancient Triticum species (Khorasan, Einkorn, Emmer), Nordic heritage landraces, and Asia-Pacific standards ($n=8$).

Each record specifies dry-basis protein (Dumas/Kjeldahl $N \times 5.70$), ash content (AACC 08-01.01), Hagberg Falling Number (AACC 56-81.03), Farinograph absorption at $500\text{ BU}$ (AACC 54-21.02), recommended sourdough hydration bounds, effective rheological hydration at $70\%$ apparent water with $20\%$ levain, corrected main water per $500\text{ g}$ main flour, Arrhenius fermentation coefficients at $18^\circ\text{C}$, $24^\circ\text{C}$, and $28^\circ\text{C}$, peak bulk fermentation duration at $24^\circ\text{C}$, and gluten extensibility index.
