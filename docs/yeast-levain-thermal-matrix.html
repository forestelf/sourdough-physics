1. Bidirectional Yeast ↔ Sourdough Levain Mass Conservation Equations

Commercial yeast (whether Instant Dry Yeast at ~95% dry solids, Active Dry Yeast requiring thermal rehydration, or Fresh Compressed Cake Yeast at ~70% moisture) contributes negligible flour or free water to a dough formula. In contrast, a sourdough levain is a hydrated flour-and-water suspension. When using our Bidirectional Yeast to Sourdough Starter Converter, the mass of fermented flour ($F_{\text{lev}}$) and starter water ($W_{\text{lev}}$) inside a levain of total mass $L$ and hydration percentage $H_s$ (expressed as a decimal, e.g., $0.50$, $1.00$, or $1.25$) is governed by:

F_lev = L / (1 + H_s)   |   W_lev = L - F_lev = L * (H_s / (1 + H_s))
Adjusted_Main_Flour = F_original - F_lev   |   Adjusted_Main_Water = W_original - W_lev

Across different starter maintenance regimes—50% Stiff Pasta Madre (which promotes heterofermentative acetic acid and strengthens gluten via reduced protease activity), 100% Liquid Levain, and 125% High-Hydration Levain (which accelerates lactic acid production and extensibility)—failing to deduct $W_{\text{lev}}$ and $F_{\text{lev}}$ from the main dough shifts true hydration by up to 4.2%.

Frequently Asked Questions (Bread Physics & Conversion Reference)

How do you convert instant dry yeast (IDY) to 100% hydration sourdough starter without changing dough hydration?

Under standard artisan inoculation physics, 1 gram of Instant Dry Yeast (IDY) provides equivalent leavening power over a 4-to-5 hour bulk fermentation to approximately 28–30 grams of mature 100%-hydration sourdough starter (or 20% baker's inoculation relative to base flour). Because 100% hydration starter is 50% flour and 50% water by weight, you must subtract half of the added starter weight from the recipe's main flour and half from the recipe's main water to lock True Total Hydration.

What is the conversion ratio between Instant Yeast, Active Dry Yeast, and Fresh Cake Yeast?

By viable cell density and dry matter mass: 1.00 g of Instant Dry Yeast (IDY / Osmotolerant) equals 1.25 g of Active Dry Yeast (ADY) and 3.00 g of Fresh Compressed Cake Yeast (a 100 : 125 : 300 mass ratio).

Why does target aliquot rise percentage drop from 75% at 68°F to 30% at 80°F before cold retard?

Warm dough (80°F–84°F) retains high internal thermal momentum when placed in a 38°F–39°F refrigerator, taking 4 to 6 hours for its core temperature to drop below 45°F where wild yeast dormancy occurs. Calling bulk fermentation at 30%–40% volumetric aliquot rise at 80°F prevents over-proofing and gluten proteolysis during the first 4 hours of cold retardation.
