# Sourdough Crumb Doctor Troubleshooting Chart: Underproofed vs Overproofed vs Gummy

## Direct Answer (BLUF)

A **dense, gummy base with a few huge tunnels right under the crust** (Fool's Crumb) means bulk fermentation was cut **1.5–3 hours short** — keep going until the dough rises **50–60% at 75–78°F**. A **flat, slack loaf with a shaggy wet crumb and sharply acetic smell** means proteolytic overproofing: acids have cleaved the gluten bonds and no oven spring can recover it. A **gummy paste core inside a perfect golden crust** is not fermentation at all — you sliced **under 45 minutes** out of the oven before starch retrogradation finished. Cool every loaf **60–90 minutes** before cutting.

## Table 1: The Four Verdicts of the Crumb Doctor

| Diagnosis | Crumb Signature | Root Cause | Proven Fix |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Fool's Crumb (Underproofed)** | Dense gummy base + few oversized tunnels directly under the crust | Bulk cut 1.5–3 h short of the gas-retention window at your dough temperature | Extend bulk; stop at +50–60% volume rise at 75–78°F, not the clock |
| **Optimal Fermentation** | Glossy open alveoli with thin walls, even spring, moderate tang | Arrhenius window hit: ~4.5–5.5 h at 75°F with 20% starter inoculation | Repeat exactly: DDT 76°F, 4 coil folds, 50–55% rise, 12–16 h retard |
| **Proteolytic Collapse (Overproofed)** | Flat slack loaf, shaggy wet crumb structure, acetic / ammonia aroma | Bulk ran 1.5x+ past the window; bacterial proteases cleaved disulfide gluten bonds | Cut bulk by ~1 h; cool the kitchen; rely on the 12–16 h cold retard |
| **Early-Slicing Gumminess** | Gummy paste core beneath a picture-perfect crisp crust | Knife entered under 45 min from the oven; starch still fully gelatinized | Cool 60–90 min so amylose retrogradation sets the crumb |
